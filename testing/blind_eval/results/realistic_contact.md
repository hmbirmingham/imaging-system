# Realistic-contact evaluation

Hynes Birmingham, Ryberg Lab. Branch `feat/realistic-contact-eval`.

Status: **complete.** The decision rule was committed before Phase 3 ran
(commit `68a6ed5`) and was not changed afterward.

**Outcome: method holds.** Pooled F1 0.966 on `contact_r3` and 0.968 on
`contact_dense_r3`, both above the 0.90 threshold.

## Why I am running this

My blind eval is synthetic-only, and the generator renders forced-touching
colonies in a way real colonies do not grow. Two specific problems:

1. **Contact depth.** A forced-touching colony is placed at a centre
   distance of `(r1 + r2) * (1 - U(0.3, 0.7))`, so 30 to 70% of the radius
   sum. Real colonies expand until their edges meet and then stop, which
   puts contact near `r1 + r2`, not at half of it.
2. **No edge cue.** Every colony is drawn with one flat fill value, so two
   colonies that meet render as a single indivisible region. Under real
   lighting their touching edges leave a visible boundary line. The
   pipeline is therefore being asked to split a shape that contains no
   evidence a split is warranted.

The consequence is measurable. Scoring each colony's `visible_fraction`,
the share of its own disc not covered by any other disc:

| set | colonies | under 10% visible | under 25% visible | exactly 0% visible |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `dense_pack` | 4931 | 5.05% | 8.86% | 2.43% |
| `high_touching` | 1680 | 6.73% | 12.26% | 3.21% |

A colony at 0% visible contributes no silhouette area that another colony
does not already account for. No segmentation method can recover it, and
ground truth still counts it. So part of my recall gap is unreachable by
any method, and I cannot currently tell how much.

This test separates "my method fails" from "my generator is unfair."

## Method

Four-step ladder, 50 plates per step, holding colony count and touching
rate constant and changing only how contact is placed and rendered.

| step | rendering |
|---|---|
| `r0` | current deep-overlap rendering (control) |
| `r1` | `abut`: contact distance `(r1 + r2) * U(0.90, 1.00)` |
| `r2` | `abut` + 1.5px rim ring, 25 intensity units darker |
| `r3` | `abut` + rim ring + dome shading + 0.8px blur + sensor noise sd 2.0 |

Run twice: `contact_r0` to `r3` match `high_touching`'s colony count and
touching rate, `contact_dense_r0` to `r3` match `dense_pack`'s.

Seed base 2,000,000, disjoint from the held-out sets' 900,000 range. The
existing harness scores these unchanged: ground truth is loaded only after
detection, one-to-one greedy nearest-centroid matching, 1.5x radius gate,
pooled micro-averaged scoring. No `quantify.py` default was changed on
this branch.

Caveat on the ladder, stated before results: `r0` to `r1` changes two
things at once, contact placement and rasterisation, because sub-pixel
centres are only used in the new modes. `r1` to `r2` to `r3` each change
one thing.

## Pre-registered decision rule

Copied verbatim from the test plan, fixed before Phase 3 ran. The rule
applies to `contact_r3` and `contact_dense_r3`.

- **Method holds:** pooled F1 at least 0.90 on both. My read: the
  deep-overlap generator was the ceiling, and real plates are the only
  blocker.
- **Method holds on what's visible:** F1 below 0.90, but at least 80% of
  the misses are colonies with `visible_fraction` under 0.25. My read:
  same as above, and I report it with the visibility breakdown.
- **Real weakness:** F1 below 0.90, and the misses are mostly visible
  colonies. My read: a method problem. I record which part of the ladder
  (r1, r2 or r3) caused the drop. That points at the first fix to try: a
  dilution protocol, then concave-point splitting or multi-scale seeds,
  then a learned detector trained on real labelled plates.

## What the ladder shows

**The entire jump is at r0 to r1.** F1 goes 0.870 to 0.961 on the
high_touching family and 0.906 to 0.972 on the dense family when contact
moves from deep overlap to abutting. Adding the rim ring (r2) and then
shading, blur and noise (r3) changes nothing material: 0.961, 0.958,
0.966 and 0.972, 0.971, 0.968. Contact depth was the whole effect.

**The rim cue bought nothing, and that is informative.** I added it
expecting it to help, because a visible boundary line is exactly the
evidence a splitter should use. It did not, and the reason is structural:
`_subtract_background` thresholds to a binary mask before anything splits,
so by the time `_apply_watershed` runs, the seam has been thrown away. The
pipeline segments a silhouette, not an image. Any future method that wants
to use image evidence inside a cluster has to read it before the
threshold, not after.

**Class A disappears under realistic contact.** There were 85 and 239
isolated class A pairs at r0 and zero at r1 through r3. The condition for
a second distance-transform peak, `d^2 > r1^2 - r2^2`, is satisfied
automatically once `d` is near `r1 + r2`. What I had been calling an
architectural ceiling for distance-transform watershed is an artifact of
the generator's deep overlap, not a property of colonies that meet by
growing into each other. Class D, which splits at 95 to 98%, becomes the
dominant class instead.

**Precision degrades slightly and in a benign direction.** Pooled
precision falls from 0.998 at r0 to 0.990 to 0.996 at r1 to r3. Across all
eight sets, false positives on bare agar total 3; everything else is an
oversplit inside a real colony. The pipeline is not inventing colonies, it
is occasionally cutting one in two.

**The residual is no longer mostly unrecoverable.** At r0, 51.9% of misses
were colonies under 0.25 visible. At r3 that is 21.8%, so roughly 78% of
what is still missed is a reasonably visible colony. Recall at 0.50 and
above visible is 0.959 to 0.965, meaning about 4% of plainly visible
colonies are still missed. That is the honest remaining gap, and it is
small enough that it is not worth chasing against synthetic data.

## Limits

- **Still synthetic.** This says nothing about real-plate accuracy. It
  says my generator was the binding constraint on two of five held-out
  sets, not my method. Every number here describes renders.
- **The abut model is itself an assumption.** I chose `U(0.90, 1.00)` of
  the radius sum because colonies grow until they touch and stop. Whether
  colonies on the lab's media actually behave that way, or flatten and
  deform where they meet, is a question for Dr. Ryberg and the answer
  could move these numbers either way. Chacon et al. found colony size
  tracks each colony's Voronoi territory, which supports abutting contact
  over interpenetration, but that is a different organism and medium.
- **r0 to r1 moves two things**, contact placement and sub-pixel
  rasterisation. The r1 to r3 steps are clean single changes.
- **The ladder parameters were set, not tuned.** Rim width and delta, blur
  sigma and noise sd were chosen from physical reasoning and written into
  the generator before Phase 3 ran. No value was selected by looking at a
  score.
- **Class C is thinly populated** under abut, 5 to 13 pairs per set, so
  its low split rate there is not a reliable estimate.
- **No `quantify.py` default changed on this branch.** The pipeline scored
  here is byte-identical to the one behind the committed baseline.
